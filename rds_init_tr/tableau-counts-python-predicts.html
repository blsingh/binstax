Tableau Counts. Python Predicts.

Executive summary: what Python access would add to our reporting

Bottom line: Tableau tells us what already happened. Python can tell us what is likely to happen next, who it is likely to happen to, and whether our changes are working.

Two different jobs

Tableau: counting

  • How many incidents last month?
  • What is the census by unit?
  • How does this quarter compare to last?
  • Clear dashboards anyone can read

Python: predicting

  • Which patients' risk of an incident is rising right now?
  • What care will an aging, long-term population need in five years?
  • Where will staffing run short before overtime has to be mandated?
  • Did a program change work, or would the numbers have dropped anyway?

Why Tableau stops at counting

Tableau was built to display data, and it does that very well. Its built-in forecast takes one number over time and extends the line forward. It cannot weigh several factors together (staffing, acuity, legal status, time since admission) or make a prediction about an individual patient. For that kind of work, Tableau's own answer is to connect it to Python.

Most of these questions need data from outside the patient record. Linking more sources, such as staffing, injuries, transports, and pharmacy, is what makes the predictions possible.

"What analysis could you do?"

The honest answer depends on what is in our data, and nobody can know that until someone looks. That first look is itself the work Python makes possible:

  1. Find out what the data can support. Check which records are complete, how far back they go, and what they can be linked to.
  2. Test one question against the past. Build a prediction using older data, then check it against what actually happened. This proves whether it works before anyone relies on it.
  3. Report what works, and what doesn't. A result that works becomes a tool leadership can use. A result that doesn't tells us exactly what data we would need to collect.

In both cases, the department learns something it cannot learn from counts alone.

No prediction is perfect. But a prediction tested against our own history is far more reliable than a guess made from counts, and we know how accurate it is before anyone uses it.
